Problem 32

Melissa participates in a lottery in which she selects 7 numbers between 1 and 50, and then a computer randomly picks 7 numbers between 1 and 50 (without replacement). What is the probability that her selected numbers match exactly 5 of the randomly selected numbers, in any order?

There are ∣S∣=(507)|S| = \binom{50}{7} possible outcomes (7 distinct numbers, without order and without replacement).

To count the outcomes with exactly 5 correct numbers:

  • Choose 5 of the 7 correct numbers: (75)\binom{7}{5} possibilities.
  • For each, choose the remaining two numbers from the 43 wrong numbers: (432)\binom{43}{2} possibilities.
  • By the multiplication rule, there are (75)(432)\binom{7}{5}\binom{43}{2} ways to have exactly 5 correct.
P(exactly 5 matches)=(75)(432)(507)P(\text{exactly 5 matches}) = \frac{\binom{7}{5}\binom{43}{2}}{\binom{50}{7}}

P(D∣P)=P(P∣D)P(D)P(P∣D)P(D)+P(P∣Dˉ)P(Dˉ)=(0.96)(0.003)(0.96)(0.003)+(0.05)(0.997)≈0.0546P(D \mid P) = \frac{P(P \mid D)P(D)}{P(P \mid D)P(D) + P(P \mid \bar{D})P(\bar{D})} = \frac{(0.96)(0.003)}{(0.96)(0.003) + (0.05)(0.997)} \approx 0.0546

Problem 63

(The Monty Hall Problem.) You are on a game show with three doors. Behind one door is a car; behind the other two doors are goats. You choose Door 1. The host, who knows what is behind all three doors, opens Door 3 and reveals a goat. The host then gives you a choice: stay with Door 1, or switch to Door 2. How does switching affect your probability of winning?

  • (a) Switching increases my chances of winning the car.
  • (b) Switching decreases my chances of winning the car.
  • (c) Switching does not make a difference.

Answer: (a). Switching makes it better.

Let AiA_i = "car is behind Door ii" and HH = "host opens Door 3". Initially P(A1)=P(A2)=P(A3)=13P(A_1) = P(A_2) = P(A_3) = \frac{1}{3}. Since the host knows where the car is,

P(H∣A1)=12,P(H∣A2)=1,P(H∣A3)=0P(H \mid A_1) = \tfrac{1}{2}, \quad P(H \mid A_2) = 1, \quad P(H \mid A_3) = 0

By Bayes' theorem:

P(A1∣H)=(12)(13)(12)(13)+(1)(13)+(0)(13)=13P(A_1 \mid H) = \frac{(\frac{1}{2})(\frac{1}{3})}{(\frac{1}{2})(\frac{1}{3}) + (1)(\frac{1}{3}) + (0)(\frac{1}{3})} = \frac{1}{3}
P(A2∣H)=(1)(13)(12)(13)+(1)(13)+(0)(13)=23P(A_2 \mid H) = \frac{(1)(\frac{1}{3})}{(\frac{1}{2})(\frac{1}{3}) + (1)(\frac{1}{3}) + (0)(\frac{1}{3})} = \frac{2}{3}

Why isn't it 50–50? The host does not open a door at random: he knows where the car is and deliberately opens a door with a goat. Your original choice had probability 13\frac{1}{3} of being correct, and it still does after the host opens Door 3. The remaining probability 23\frac{2}{3} goes to Door 2.
